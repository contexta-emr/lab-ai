# The synthetic lab corpus — what it is and what it covers

300 lab report PDFs for 20 invented patients across five years, built to test document
extraction honestly. Every person, lab, doctor and value is fabricated; no row describes
a real person.

Generated by `corpus/generator/`, output in `corpus/data/`. Regenerate with
`python corpus/generator/build.py --patient all --fresh`.

**The point of it.** Samples from real labs only contain layouts you happen to have been
handed, and tuning against those is how a parser gets good at eight documents and no others.
This corpus is written from how Indian lab reports are actually laid out rather than from what
the pipeline currently handles. Some cases are expected to fail, and that is the point.

## What makes it hard

### Layouts (26)

```
clean_table 42 · kv_header_table 25 · dense_multipanel 23 · handwritten_values 20
continuation 20 · mixed_print_hand 19 · thermal_slip 18 · boxed_panels 17
two_column 15 · minimal_lines 13 · ledger_ruled 13 · poor_scan 11 · landscape_wide 10
typewriter_manual 9 · fax_header 8 · carbon_copy 6 · glossy_marketing 6
duplicate_watermark 5 · cut_sheet 4 · mobile_photo 3 · dot_matrix_pinfeed 3
chat_screenshot 3 · register_page 2 · discharge_summary 2 · range_bar 2
handwritten_full 1
```

Five of these are not lab reports in the usual sense:

- **`chat_screenshot`** — the lab sent values as chat messages; a phone-shaped page, bubbles,
  timestamps, a reply from the patient
- **`register_page`** — a photograph of the lab's ruled register with **four other patients'
  handwritten entries** around ours. Picking one row out of a page addressed to nobody
- **`discharge_summary`** — two pages of hospital prose with the labs buried in a section
  between the treatment and the advice
- **`range_bar`** — the modern chain-lab format where each value sits on a graphic bar instead
  of beside a printed interval
- **`thermal_slip`** — an 80 mm till roll, values under names rather than beside them

### Header styles (9)

`minimal_text_top` 63 · `letterhead_logo_left` 56 · `hospital_department_block` 40 ·
`centred_title_only` 35 · `banner_with_qr` 28 · `stamp_and_signature` 22 ·
`two_line_ribbon` 21 · `bilingual_header` 18 · `barcode_header` 17

`bilingual_header` prints the lab name in Devanagari, Gujarati, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada,
Malayalam, Bengali, Odia, Punjabi or Assamese beside the English.

### Physical damage (52 reports, 17%)

`aged_paper` 26 · `fold_creases` 25 · `poor_lighting` 12 · `stain_damage` 10 ·
`cropped_edge` 9 · `scanner_border` 6 · `blurred_photo` 5 · `finger_over_page` 3 ·
`illegible_scan` 3 · `torn_corner` 2

Confined to patients 15–20. Yellowed and foxed paper, fold creases with shadow either side,
tea rings, torn corners, staple holes, edges cropped out of a photograph, pages shot in bad
light with a thumb across the corner.

> **Every damaged page has no text layer.** The CSS filters that produce the damage force the
> browser to rasterise the page, so those 52 PDFs can only be read by OCR — `paddle_vl`
> (PaddleOCR-VL on our own hardware, about 20 minutes a page) or `openparser` (the same model
> on OpenParser's hosted GPU, about 6 seconds and roughly ₹0.09 a page, but the document
> leaves India). That is correct for a photograph or a photocopy, and deliberate — but it
> means roughly a sixth of the corpus tests the OCR path rather than layout parsing, and a
> scorer should treat them as their own bucket. The three marked `illegible_scan` can only
> ever score near zero.
>
> Being synthetic, this corpus is one of the few things that **may** go to OpenParser: the
> idp workspace restricts the hosted engine to synthetic and vendor sample documents, never a
> real patient report. Which makes these 300 PDFs the right way to benchmark it.

## What every patient is guaranteed to have

`build.py` asserts all of this and exits non-zero on failure. If it says the checks pass,
they pass.

| Rule | Guarantee |
|---|---|
| Panel coverage | Each of the ten core panels on **2 or 3** of that patient's 15 reports |
| Panel titles | Rotated — the same panel never prints the same title every time |
| Layouts · labs | 6+ layouts, 3+ labs per patient (actual: 10–15 layouts, 3–4 labs) |
| Traps | 8+ distinct per patient |
| Distinct tests | 45+ per patient |
| Health checks | 2 per patient, 5–6 panels and 25+ tests each |
| Single-test reports | 2 per patient |
| Text results | 3+ per patient |
| Uncoded tests | 4+ per patient, key records a blank code |
| Tracked analyte | On 8+ of the 15 reports |
| Derived values | Recompute exactly from the printed inputs |
| Dates | In order, inside the window, ages advancing correctly |
| Page count | One page, except `continuation` (2) and the roll/screenshot/summary formats |

Panel instances across the corpus: CBC 59 · LFT 54 · KFT 49 · LIPID 48 · DIABETES 45 ·
URINE_ROUTINE 45 · THYROID 45 · IRON 44 · ELECTROLYTES 41 · VITAMIN 41, plus INFLAMMATION 13 ·
SEROLOGY 9 · COAGULATION 5 · CARDIAC 5 · HORMONES 5 · TUMOUR_MARKERS 1.

**Read it two ways.** Every patient has **14 to 45** analytes with three or more points, so
there is plenty to chart. But only **1 to 5** analytes per patient reach six or more points,
and those are almost always the tracked disease test plus its immediate companions.

**Why.** Two rules pull against each other. The spec caps each panel at 2–3 reports, so panel
members land on 2–3 reports by construction. The tracked analyte then gets 8+ appearances as
loose standalone rows. Nothing was asked for in between, so nothing exists in between.

### The gap to 6–7 repeated tests per patient

Target: **at least 7 analytes with 6+ points each, for every patient.** What that costs, if
the closest candidates (those already at 3–5 points) are topped up:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P01 | +5 analytes | ~12 | P11 | +5 | ~6 |
| P02 | +3 | ~4 | P12 | +4 | ~6 |
| P03 | +4 | ~6 | P13 | +3 | ~8 |
| P04 | +5 | ~9 | P14 | +5 | ~6 |
| P05 | +3 | ~3 | P15 | +6 | ~12 |
| P06 | +2 | ~3 | P16 | +3 | ~4 |
| P07 | +5 | ~13 | P17 | +4 | ~6 |
| P08 | +6 | ~14 | P18 | +4 | ~8 |
| P09 | +2 | ~3 | P19 | +5 | ~9 |
| P10 | +3 | ~5 | P20 | +4 | ~9 |

**About 146 new printed rows across the corpus** — roughly 7 per patient, 0.5 per report.

**How it should be done.** As small "Follow-up" or "Repeat Investigations" blocks of two to
four rows on the reports that currently carry few tests. That is how a clinic orders a couple
of repeat tests, and — critically — **loose rows are not panel instances**, so the 2-or-3
panel rule is untouched and no existing check breaks.

**Which analytes**, chosen per patient to match the case rather than one fixed set: a CKD
patient repeats creatinine, urea, potassium, calcium, haemoglobin and eGFR; a thyroid patient
repeats TSH, FT4, cholesterol and haemoglobin; a child with anaemia repeats haemoglobin, MCV,
RDW and ferritin. A fixed set across all twenty would make every record look the same, which
is what a varied corpus exists to avoid.

**Not yet done.** This section is the plan, not the state. The numbers above describe the
corpus as it stands today.

## Vitals

One JSON file per patient, `corpus/data/vitals/PNN-vitals.json`, one row per lab date:

```json
{
  "report_id": "P01-R01", "date": "2021-10-05", "age_years": 52,
  "height_cm": 172.0, "weight_kg": 88.4, "bmi": 29.9,
  "blood_pressure": "148/94", "spo2_pct": 98, "temperature_f": 98.4,
  "context": "First visit after incidental hyperglycaemia. Overweight, hypertensive."
}
```

BMI is computed in code from height and weight, so it cannot disagree with them. Blood
pressure is the text `"148/94"` because that is what the production column holds — it is TEXT
and split at query time. Temperature is Fahrenheit, as Indian clinics record it.

**The vitals track the lab story rather than sitting beside it.** P01's weight is 88.4 kg at
diagnosis, 78.6 at his best HbA1c, back to 87.1 through the relapse. The three growing
children gain height year on year. The only febrile visits are the ones where something
actually happened.

## The sealed answer key

`corpus/data/answers/reports.csv` — one line per report:

```
report_id, pdf_path, patient_number, uhid, patient_name, sex, age_printed,
collected_at, reported_at, lab_name, uhid_label, referring_doctor, department,
expected_panels, layout_style, header_style, traps
```

`corpus/data/answers/rows.csv` — one line per printed test:

```
report_id, section_heading, printed_label, code, value, unit, reference, method, flag, trap
```

`expected_panels` and `traps` are `|`-separated. `code` is blank for a test with no code in
the vocabulary. `value` is exactly as printed — `03`, `<0.01`, `Not Detected`.

> **Nothing in the parsing path may read this.** It exists to score an extraction afterwards.
> A parser that reads the key measures nothing.

## Regenerating

```bash
python corpus/generator/build.py --patient 1 --html-only   # compose, check coverage, no PDFs
python corpus/generator/build.py --patient 1               # render and check page counts
python corpus/generator/build.py --patient all --fresh     # the whole corpus
python corpus/generator/build.py --patient 1 --only 6      # one report, while tuning a layout
python db/seed/export_master.py                            # refresh the DB analyte master
```

`corpus/generator/catalog.py` is the single source of truth for the vocabulary — panels,
analytes, aliases, units, reference ranges, derived-value maths. The database seed is
generated from it, so the corpus and the analyte master cannot drift apart.

## The holdout set

Everything above describes the 300-report **training** corpus: patients P01–P20, 26
layouts, vocabulary drawn from `catalog.py`.

A separate 15-report evaluation set was added on 2026-09-25 — three new patients,
twelve layouts that appear nowhere above, and printed labels deliberately absent from
the master. It lives in `corpus/data/holdout/` with its own sealed answer key and is
loaded only on request. See **`docs/holdout-testset.md`**.

Keep the two apart. The training corpus is what the extractor was built against; the
holdout is the only thing here that can tell you whether that building generalised.
